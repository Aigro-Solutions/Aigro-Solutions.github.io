# Abstract

QUBITS AI AUŠRA: Human Amplification in the Age of Quantum-AI Systems

This paper examines the intersection between artificial intelligence, quantum computing, and the transformation of human work. From a historical and methodological review connecting counting, logic, information, classical computation, quantum mechanics, generative AI, and autonomous agents, the study proposes a conceptual model for evaluating how automation affects tasks, professions, and human capacity. The central question is not simply whether people will be replaced by machines, but how work can be reorganized so that tasks are automated without reducing dignity, autonomy, judgment, and responsibility.

The analysis is organized into four layers: the physical foundations of quantum computing, the foundations of AI and generative models, the architecture of agents and tool-using systems, and the impact of this ecosystem on professional tasks. To do so, the paper introduces the editorial structure ENS · ARS: ENS represents the human being; ARS represents the art, technique, and craft of making. Using this frame, the paper proposes indicators of human amplification and professional continuity, focusing on how automation reorganizes functions and redefines the boundary between the automatable component and the human component of work.

The main contribution is structural: to distinguish fact, hypothesis, roadmap, and scenario, avoiding the simplification that confuses technological promise with real economic and social change. The paper concludes that the future of AI and quantum computing should not be thought of as a mere replacement of human work, but as a new architecture of collaboration where the person remains responsible for decision-making, verification, ethics, creativity, and critical judgment.

Keywords: artificial intelligence; quantum computing; human work; human amplification; generative AI; AI agents; cognitive sovereignty; future of work; quantum-AI.
